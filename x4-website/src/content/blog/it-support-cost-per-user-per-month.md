Most IT support for small businesses is sold as a price per user per month. It looks like the easiest number in the world to compare. One provider says one figure, another says a different one, and you pick the lower.

It is not that simple. Two quotes with the same number can buy very different things. And the number on the quote is often not the number on the invoice.

You will not find a "typical market rate" in this post. We have not seen one that somebody actually counted, and a guess is no help to you when you set a budget. What we can do is show you what a per-user price is made of, show you our own price worked through, and give you the questions that make two quotes comparable.

## What a per-user price is made of

A managed IT price per user usually pays for four kinds of thing.

- **Licences.** Your Microsoft 365 subscription: email, Teams, Word, Excel, OneDrive. Some providers put this inside their price. Some bill it on top.
- **Security and tools.** Antivirus, device management, multi-factor sign-in, backup, monitoring and patching software, and often a password manager and staff training. The provider pays for these per user or per device.
- **People's time.** The help desk, the engineer who fixes the problem, and the time it takes to set up new starters and shut off leavers.
- **Getting started.** Somebody has to learn how your IT is set up before they can look after it. Some providers charge a set-up fee for this. Some spread it across the contract.

Some costs do not grow with headcount at all. Your firewall, your Wi-Fi and your broadband cost the same whether six people use them or sixteen. A provider has to recover those costs somehow. Some charge a fee per office. Others hide them inside the per-user figure, which only works out fairly at one size of company.

## The licence is the biggest difference between quotes

The Microsoft 365 licence is the part of the price you can check for yourself. On Microsoft's UK website on 15 November 2026, these were the prices per user per month, paid yearly, excluding VAT:

| Plan | Price |
|---|---|
| Microsoft 365 Business Basic | £5.40 |
| Microsoft 365 Business Standard | £10.80 |
| Microsoft 365 Business Premium | £16.90 |
| Microsoft 365 Business Standard with Copilot | £18.10 |
| Microsoft 365 Business Premium with Copilot | £24.60 |

The "with Copilot" plans add Microsoft's AI assistant. Microsoft's comparison page shows only those versions of Standard and Premium. The plans without Copilot are on each plan's own page.

That is a difference of £19.20 per user per month between the cheapest and the most complete of these five plans. So before you compare two IT support quotes, ask two questions. Is the licence inside the price? And if it is, which one?

The plan matters for more than price. Microsoft says Business Premium adds advanced protection against cyber threats and device management to the apps in Business Standard. Those are the tools that let a provider wipe a lost laptop or stop a ransomware attack. A provider who quotes low on Business Basic still needs tools to do that job. They have to buy them somewhere, and you pay for them somewhere.

Now divide that by the 15 people. It comes to about **£75.67 per person**, not £65. That is the honest figure to put next to another provider's quote, because it is what you actually pay per head.

The office fee matters more in a small firm. With 6 people in one office, the total is £550 a month, about £91.67 per person. With 30 people, it is £2,110 a month, about £70.33 per person.

We show you this because every provider has some version of it. If a quote has only one number, ask where the office costs went.

## Why two quotes with the same number are not the same

When you have two or three quotes in front of you, check these lines on each one.

- **Licence in or out.** And which plan, as above.
- **Per user or per device.** One person with a laptop, a desktop and a phone can count as three devices.
- **Office and network costs.** A separate fee, inside the per-user price, or not covered at all?
- **Set-up fee.** Is there one, and how much is it?
- **Minimum term and notice.** How long you are tied in, and how much notice you must give to leave.
- **On-site visits.** Included, or charged per visit and per mile?
- **Cover hours.** Office hours only, or evenings and weekends too? If you need more, what does it cost?
- **Response times.** Are they in the contract? A response time is when somebody starts work on your problem, not when it is fixed.
- **What is not covered.** Hardware, projects, servers, extra Microsoft licences. Every provider has a list. Ask for it in writing.

## Costs that look cheap now and dear later

Some prices are low on the quote and high on the invoice. Watch for these.

- **A low price per user with a large set-up fee.** Add the set-up fee to the first year before you compare.
- **"Unlimited support" with limits nobody wrote down.** Ask what happens on the third visit in a month, or the tenth call.
- **Licences for people who have left.** If nobody reviews your licences, you keep paying for former staff. Ask who checks, and how often.
- **Backups that nobody tests.** A backup only counts if somebody has restored from it. Ask how often they test, and how they prove it.

## How to compare quotes properly

Give every provider the same facts: how many people, how many offices, and how many computers and phones. Then, for each quote:

1. Add up everything you would pay in the first term, including any set-up fee and office fees.
2. Divide that by the number of months in the term.
3. Divide again by the number of people.

That gives you one figure per person per month for each quote. Now set that next to what each provider covers and what each one leaves out. The cheapest figure is only cheaper if it buys the same thing.
