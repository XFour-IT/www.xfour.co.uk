The short answer: the licences have a published price, and the rest does not.

Microsoft publishes what it charges per user per month, so you can work out that part today. The cost of setting Dynamics 365 up for your business depends on your business. Anyone who gives you a firm figure for that before they have seen how you work is guessing.

This post gives you the part we can count, and an honest account of the part we cannot.

## First, which Dynamics 365?

Dynamics 365 is a family of products, not one product. Small businesses usually look at one of three:

- **Business Central** runs the business: finance, sales orders, purchasing, stock and projects. If you are coming off spreadsheets or an old accounts package, this is usually the one.
- **Sales** manages customers and the sales pipeline: leads, opportunities, quotes and forecasts.
- **Customer Service** handles cases, queues and a knowledge base for a support team.

Some businesses need two of them. Many need only one. A wrong choice here costs more than any licence decision, so make this choice first.

## Not everybody needs a full licence

This is where a small business can save the most money. It is also where it most often goes wrong.

In Business Central, a full user on Essentials or Premium can do everything that licence covers. A Team Members user costs much less, but Microsoft limits what that user can do: read data, approve workflows, and update some records. It is not a cheap way to give people full access.

So the real question is not "how many staff do we have?" It is "how many people do their daily work in the system?"

Here is an example, worked from the prices above. It is arithmetic, not a quote. Take a 20-person business on Business Central Essentials:

- **If all 20 people need full access:** 20 × £61.50 = £1,230 a month, or £14,760 a year.
- **If 8 people do the daily work and 12 only look things up and approve:** (8 × £61.50) + (12 × £6.20) = £566.40 a month, or £6,796.80 a year.

Same business, same product. The second licence bill is less than half the first. Which one is yours depends on what each person actually does. That is worth an hour of honest thought before you buy anything.

Two more points on Business Central:

- **Premium adds service management and manufacturing.** If you do not use them, you do not need Premium.
- **Essentials users cannot sign in to a company set up for Premium.** If one part of your business needs Premium features, plan for this when you set up your companies, [as Microsoft describes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/ui-how-users-permissions), not after.

## What the licence price does not include

The licence is the part you can count. These are the parts no price list shows:

- **Setting it up.** Configuring the system for your processes, your accounts and your approval rules. This is partner work, and in the first year it is usually the largest cost.
- **Moving your data.** The time this takes depends on how much data you have and how clean it is. Five years of spreadsheets with three versions of every customer is a different job from one tidy accounts package.
- **Connecting other systems.** Your online shop, your payroll, your bank feeds, your warehouse. Each connection is work.
- **Add-ons.** Apps from other companies for things Dynamics does not do as standard. Each one has its own price.
- **Copilot agents.** Some AI agent features use Copilot Credits, which Microsoft sells separately. Find out which features you will actually use.
- **Training and support after go-live.** People need to learn the new system, and somebody has to answer their questions in the second month.

## Why we do not publish a range for the rest

You will find posts that say a small business implementation "typically" costs between one figure and another. We do not publish one.

A range is only honest if somebody counted the projects behind it. The ranges online rarely say what they counted. Your cost depends on your business, so we would rather tell you what changes it:

- **How many processes go live first.** Finance and orders first, then stock, then projects, costs less up front than all of it at once.
- **How clean your data is.** Clean data moves quickly. Messy data must be fixed first, and somebody has to decide which version is true.
- **How many systems must connect.** Each one adds work.
- **How much you change the product.** Changing the standard product costs more now, and it costs again at every upgrade. Where you can, change your process to fit the product. Customise only where your process is what makes you money.
- **How much your own people can do.** Your staff know your data and your processes better than any partner. The more of that work they can take on, the less you pay us for it.
